Overview

How can genetic perturbations propagate through gene regulatory, RNA processing, protein homeostasis, and stress response networks to alter neuronal state and function?

My work combines experimental neuroscience and computational biology. I use human stem cell models, transcriptomics, quantitative imaging, and reproducible analysis workflows to connect molecular mechanisms with systems-level changes in neurodegeneration.

Research

Inherited neurodegenerative disease

I investigate inherited forms of neurodegeneration, including ALS and repeat expansion disorders, as models for understanding how pathogenic genetic variation alters neuronal molecular programs and disease-relevant cellular states.

SOD1-associated ALS

In genome-edited isogenic iPSC-derived motor neurons, I examine how ALS-associated SOD1 mutations influence transcriptional dynamics, neuronal maturation, RNA metabolism, and mutation-specific molecular heterogeneity.

Repeat expansion disorders

I am interested in repeat expansion disorders as a context for studying how inherited genetic variation may affect molecular networks and cellular homeostasis in neurodegenerative disease.

Projects

SOD1-associated ALS

SOD1-associated ALS: genetic heterogeneity and temporal molecular remodeling

This work combines a systematic review of SOD1-associated ALS with isogenic iPSC-derived motor neuron models to connect clinical and genetic heterogeneity with mutation-specific transcriptional changes during neuronal differentiation and maturation.
